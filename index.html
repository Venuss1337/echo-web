ECHO, a local-first music player for Windows

A music player for the records you keep

Press the paper and it answers. Drag, and it opens. Press the paper. It answers.

Somewhere on your drive there’s a folder nobody else has

The bootleg. The demo a friend sent once. The album that left every catalogue and never came back.

ECHO is built around that folder. Nothing is uploaded, nothing expires, and nobody asks for your email.

A record is a place before it is a file.

Instruments float. Music doesn’t.

Play, seek and volume hover over the page. The record stays put, square-cornered, and lights everything else. That is the idea behind Atmosphere 2.0, ECHO’s interface.

Change the record and the room changes

ECHO takes three colours from each sleeve.

one for the room

one for its glow

one for the controls

Each is checked for contrast before any type touches it.

Press anywhere to play it.

Point it at your folders

FLAC, MP3, WAV, OGG, AAC and M4A, read where they already are. ECHO leaves the files alone.

Unplug the drive and your playlists, favourites and history stay, waiting for it.

Radio is the one part that isn’t yours

So it is the one part of this page that won’t hold still. It’s live.

Search the open Radio Browser directory or paste a stream address, MP3 or AAC. Your own queue stays where you left it.

Then it makes room

The compact edition. A sleeve, a title, a groove. The rest of the desk is yours.

The pattern is public

ECHO is free software, GPL-3.0-or-later, written in Rust and TypeScript. Every cut is in the open.

The records on this page are made up. The interface isn’t: every screen is a capture of ECHO’s own front end.
